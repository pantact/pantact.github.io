Abstract

Humanoid robots have many degrees of freedom and contact surfaces, yet most loco-manipulation research restricts manipulation to the hands and avoids other body contacts with the environment. To extend manipulation beyond the hands, we present PanTact, a system that learns humanoid whole-body dexterity, the capability to manipulate with any suitable body surface, directly from robot-free human demonstrations. Our key idea is to use contact as manipulation-centric information that transfers across embodiments. Contact locations and timing specify the interactions needed to accomplish a task, while the robot chooses how to coordinate its body to realize them within its own physical constraints. Specifically, PanTact captures, learns, and executes whole-body contact via: 1) a wearable, robot-free data collection interface that augments egocentric vision and bimanual UMI grippers with whole-body modular contact patches to record body contact locations and states; 2) a visuomotor policy that mitigates the egocentric observation gap and predicts whole-body contact goals; and 3) contact-aware whole-body control formulations that realize active contact goals using suitable robot surfaces. We deploy PanTact on a Unitree G1 humanoid without additional body contact sensors, demonstrating whole-body dexterity involving body support, pushing, sweeping, and load-sharing.

Wearable Whole-Body Contact Interface

Egocentric-augmented bimanual UMI grippers plus modular contact patches on the forearms and knees record where, when, and how hard the demonstrator's body contacts the environment.

  • Tile-like modules. Each 44×44 mm module carries a single force-sensitive resistor (FSR). A custom PCB streams up to 16 FSR channels at 1 kHz; modules are daisy-chained over an RS-485 bus.
  • Magnetic tiling. Press-fit connectors reinforced by magnets let patches of any shape be assembled by snapping modules together. Strap connectors secure the patch to the body, and tracker connectors mount an HTC Vive tracker to track the patch pose.
  • Contact detection & localization. Contact is binarized against a per-channel adaptive baseline to handle FSR drift, and each sensor's 3D position follows from a rigid transform of its patch tracker.
  • Vision & pose. A head-mounted stereo camera and two fisheye wrist cameras at 60 fps, Vive trackers on the head and wrists, and gripper widths, all logged on a shared clock.

Whole-Body Contact Policy

A contact-predictive policy maps embodiment-agnostic observations to EEF trajectories and whole-body contact goals.

  • Geometric body masking. Each gripper frame and contact patch defines a body-facing half-space; their intersection approximates the demonstrator's body, which is masked in the attention layers of the egocentric pointmap encoder. No appearance model of either embodiment is needed, and it scales to any number of patches.
  • Contact-aware action space. Each action step contains a head look-at point, EEF poses and gripper widths, and per-sensor contact goals, each a binary contact state and a 3D contact position.
  • Active vs. inactive goals. Only active goals request the robot to make contact; inactive goals do not force the robot to reproduce the human's free-space body motion, leaving the controller free to choose a robot-suited configuration.

Contact-Aware Whole-Body Control

The controller realizes active contact goals with any suitable robot surface, rather than assigning each contact to a fixed link.

  • Any-surface contact objective. The distance from a commanded contact to the robot is a soft-min over all candidate bodies, so whichever surface can best reach the contact satisfies it:
  • Virtual contact targets. Active contact targets are offset into the object along the pressing direction, so the residual tracking error produces a pressing force at contact.

Three Controller Formulations

  • Upper-Body IK. Differential IK QP jointly optimizing EEF and contact tasks. Requires no training data or simulation; limited to upper-body tasks with fixed legs.
  • WBIK + SONIC. Contact-aware whole-body IK whose dense reference is executed by the existing SONIC motion tracker. Enables lower-body contacts and locomotion.
  • Sparse Contact Tracker. RL goal-conditioned controller that directly tracks sparse EEF and contact goals, trained in simulation with hindsight contact labeling.

Sweep-and-Stow

The robot sweeps tall objects from a counter into an open bin in a single stroke, then uses a lower-body surface to push the cabinet closed. Ours (WBIK + SONIC) reaches 90.63% end-to-end success; Humanoid UMI++ 0%.

Grasp-and-Embrace

The robot grasps a small object with the gripper, then embraces a bulky object with its arms and moves both simultaneously. Ours (Upper-Body IK) reaches 85% success; Humanoid UMI 0%.

Carry-and-Push

The robot carries a box in both arms, walks towards a larger box on the floor, and pushes it with its shins and knees. Ours (WBIK + SONIC) reaches 90% success; Humanoid UMI++ 40%.
